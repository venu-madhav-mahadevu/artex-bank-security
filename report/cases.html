Korean banks — breached through the edges

Peripheral systems, not core banking · data exposed, no funds taken

Entry

Attackers probed peripheral systems (partner/support portals), not the hardened core banking apps.

Weaknesses chained

SQL injection and an authentication bypass in those side systems — both known, preventable classes.

Impact

~67,000 people's data exposed. Notably, no money was taken and customer banking wasn't breached.

Why it worked

The crown-jewel apps were well defended; the forgotten edges weren't. Automation found the weak edge fast.

Lesson: your attack surface is every internet-facing app, including partner portals, support tools and legacy side-systems. Inventory and defend the edges as if they were the core — see the self-assessment.

ConnectWise / Storm-0940 — the OAuth blast radius

Password spray → over-privileged vendor apps · 2,250+ tenants

Foothold

A password-spray against accounts with weak/no MFA gave initial access.

Pivot to OAuth

The actor abused over-privileged vendor OAuth apps — signing in as the app, which skips the user MFA prompt entirely.

Scale

Using stolen app credentials, access fanned out across 2,250+ tenants at once.

Why it worked

Trusted third-party apps held broad scopes, and nobody alerted on new app credentials or service-principal sign-ins.

Lesson: MFA on humans isn't enough if a trusted app can sign in as itself with broad scopes. Review OAuth consents, grant least privilege, and alert on new app credentials.

What both teach

  • No zero-days needed. Known, preventable weaknesses were enough once chained at speed.
  • The weakest edge decides the outcome — not your best-defended app.
  • Identity is the new perimeter. Credentials, tokens and app permissions are the real targets.
  • Detection speed matters. Machine-speed attacks need machine-speed alerting and containment.
